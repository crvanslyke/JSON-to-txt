Adaptation for Rice & Crawfish Farmers
Weather‑Smart Strategies for the Acadiana Region
Louisiana Sea Grant – Helping You thrive in a changing climate
 1. What’s Changing and Why It Matters
 Climate Trend  How it shows up in Acadiana  Impact on Your Rice‑Crawfish Rotation
 Higher Summer Temps  80–90 °F for longer periods  Rice growth slows, more “water‑stress” days; crawfish grow faster but may become stressed if water is too hot.
 Variable Rainfall  Heavy downpours followed by long dry spells  Flood timing becomes harder; excess water can drown rice seedlings, while droughts shrink the pool for crawfish.
 Storm Frequency & Intensity  More Category‑1/2 hurricanes and tropical storms  Sudden flooding can damage rice fields; wind damage to cages, increased salinity from storm surge.
 Sea‑Level Rise & Salt Intrusion  Higher tides bring salt into low‑lying fields  Rice varieties may suffer from salinity; crawfish prefer lower salinity, but some species can tolerate a bit more.
These changes mean the “one‑size‑fits‑all” calendar that worked for decades may no longer be reliable. The key is to keep the rhythm of planting, flooding, and harvesting while staying flexible enough to respond to the weather.
 2. Practical Adaptation Strategies
 A. Adjust Your Calendar
 Plant earlier or later to avoid the hottest part of summer for rice.
  - Do: Start with a “warm‑season” seedling kit that matures quicker.
  - Don’t: Stick to the same dates every year if the weather is different.
 Use heat‑tolerant rice varieties (e.g., “Dwarf 90” or local hybrids that finish in ~120 days).
  - These varieties keep yields steady even when temperatures climb.
 B. Water Management
 Flood Timing
  - Flood just before a forecasted rainstorm to avoid excess water.
  - During droughts, use “partial flooding” – keep the field at a shallow depth to reduce evaporation.
 Cage Placement
  - Place cages on raised platforms or use floating systems.
  - This protects against sudden water level changes and keeps crawfish out of the rice root zone during heavy rains.
 C. Diversify & Protect
 Add a short‑duration cover crop (e.g., millet or sorghum) before the rice cycle.
  - It protects soil, reduces erosion from heavy rains, and can be harvested for feed or mulch.
 Use windbreaks (rows of tall shrubs) around the perimeter.
  - They reduce wind damage to cages and lower evaporative loss from the pool.
 D. Leverage Local Knowledge
 Share field observations with neighbors each season.
  - If one farm notices early flooding, others can adjust theirs.
 Keep a “weather diary.”
  - Record rainfall totals, high/low temps, and field conditions.
  - Over time you’ll spot patterns that help plan future cycles.
 3. Decision Framework: When to Flood & Harvest
 Situation  Suggested Action  Why It Works
 Forecasted heavy rain > 1 in (25 mm) in <48 hrs  Flood the rice field before the storm.  Prevents over‑flooding and root damage.
 Rainfall < 0.5 in (12 mm) in a dry season  Maintain partial flooding (~2–3 ft) and consider supplemental irrigation if a drought is expected.  Keeps rice roots moist while conserving water for crawfish.
 High Temp (> 90 °F) and low humidity  Harvest rice early (within the last week of the growing period).  Avoids heat‑stress on grains and preserves water for late‑season crawfish.
 Storm surge expected  Move cages to higher ground or use floating platforms; flood rice fields only if the water level is stable.  Protects both crops from sudden saltwater intrusion and physical damage.
Quick Checklist for Farmers
- Before Flooding:
  - Check the latest rainfall forecast.
  - Measure current field depth; aim for 2–3 ft (rice) and 4–6 ft (crawfish pool).
- During the Cycle:
  - Monitor temperatures daily; if > 90 °F for 3 days, consider temporary shading (tarps) over rice paddies.
- Before Harvest:
  - Count days to expected storm.
  - If a storm is likely within 5 days, harvest rice sooner and secure crawfish cages.
 4. Your Next Steps
1. Review the calendar with your family and adjust planting dates based on last year’s weather patterns.
2. Talk to neighbors – share observations and plan a “rain‑ready” strategy for the region.
3. Experiment with one heat‑tolerant rice variety this season and note the results.
4. Keep a simple log on paper or a phone app – note rainfall, temperature, flooding depth, and harvest dates.
5. Reach out to your local Louisiana Sea Grant office or Extension agent for tailored advice and sample seed.
Remember – adaptation is a team effort. Small, thoughtful changes can keep your rice and crawfish thriving for generations.
