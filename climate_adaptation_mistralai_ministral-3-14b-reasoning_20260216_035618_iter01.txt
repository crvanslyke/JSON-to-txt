Adapting to Climate Change: Protecting Your Rice-Crawfish Farm’s Future
Louisiana Sea Grant  For Rural Farmers in Acadiana
 Why Adaptation Matters for Your Farm
You’ve spent generations perfecting your rice-crawfish rotation—flooding fields, managing water, and balancing profits. But Louisiana’s climate is changing, bringing more unpredictable rain, hotter summers, and rising sea levels. These shifts can disrupt your cycle if you don’t adjust.
Here’s how to stay ahead without overhauling what works for your family:
 1. Weather Changes & Your Farm
What’s at Stake?
- More Rain, More Flooding: Heavy downpours (like in 2023) can delay planting or drown crops if drainage isn’t managed.
- Hotter Droughts: Longer dry spells stress rice and crawfish, requiring extra irrigation—and more fuel to pump water.
- Salty Intrusion: Rising saltwater levels threaten soil health, especially near coastal fields.
How It Affects Your Cycle:
 Challenge  Impact on Rice-Crawfish Farming
 Early Flooding  Drowns crawfish before harvest; delays rice planting.
 Late Flooding  Crawfish may not breed well; rice may get too mature for market.
 Drought Stress  Rice yields drop; crawfish seek deeper water, reducing catches.
 Saltwater Creep  Soil becomes less fertile over time; affects both crops.
 2. Practical Adaptation Strategies
A. Water Management Upgrades (Low-Cost, High-Impact)
- Install Flexible Drainage: Add temporary drainage pipes to handle sudden downpours without ruining fields.
  - Why? Prevents waterlogging during heavy rains but allows flooding for crawfish.
- Use Rain Sensors: Cheap sensors (like those used in irrigation) alert you when rain is coming, helping you adjust flooding timing.
  - Local Example: Some farmers in St. Mary Parish now use them to avoid overflooding.
B. Crop Timing Adjustments
- Plant Rice Earlier: Start rice slightly sooner if early rains threaten. This gives crawfish more time to establish before the next flood.
- Stagger Crawfish Harvests: Instead of one big harvest, split it into two to hedge against early flooding.
  - Tip: Watch local weather forecasts (like [LSU AgCenter’s alerts](https://www.lsuagcenter.com)) for rain predictions.
C. Soil & Water Resilience
- Test Your Soil: Check salt levels if you’re near coastal areas. LSU AgCenter offers free soil tests.
  - Pro Move: If salt is a problem, mix in gypsum (a common amendment) to flush it out.
- Diversify Water Sources: If pumps are failing, explore backup options like community water hubs or solar-powered pumps.
D. Insurance & Emergency Plans
- Farmers’ Markets Insurance: The [Louisiana Crop Insurance Program](https://www.lsuagcenter.com/agriculture/farm-business/insurance) now covers more climate risks.
- Emergency Drainage Plan: Know where to direct excess water in case of levee failures.
 3. Smart Timing Decisions: Flooding & Harvesting
When to Flood for Crawfish:
- Ideal Window: After rice harvest (typically late summer/fall), flood fields after the first hard frost (crawfish prefer cooler water).
  - Climate Adjustment: If summers stay hotter, consider flooding in early October to avoid extreme heat stress.
When to Drain for Rice:
- Drought Watch: If rain is scarce, drain fields earlier to save irrigation water. Plant rice when soil moisture is optimal (not too dry, not too wet).
  - Tool: Use a soil moisture sensor or your thumb test: Push a finger 2 inches into the soil—if it’s dry, time to irrigate.
Harvesting Crawfish:
- Rainy Day Strategy: If heavy rain is forecasted during harvest season, set traps in areas that drain slower (e.g., field edges) and avoid low-lying spots.
  - Pro Tip: Use floating traps in deeper water if flooding is unpredictable.
 4. Local Expertise & Resources
You don’t have to figure this out alone:
- LSU AgCenter Agents: They visit farms weekly—ask about [climate adaptation workshops](https://www.lsuagcenter.com) in your parish.
- Farmers’ Coops: Groups like the [Acadia Rice Producers Association](https://www.acadiarice.com) share weatherproofing tips from peers.
- Louisiana Sea Grant: Free fact sheets on [rice-crawfish climate resilience](https://laseagrant.org).
Quick Checklist:
✅ Test soil for salt if you’re near the coast.
✅ Install a rain sensor or drainage pipe this off-season.
✅ Sign up for LSU AgCenter weather alerts.
✅ Talk to your local agent about crop insurance options.
 Final Thought: Adapt, Don’t Overhaul
You’ve already adapted—from hand-planting rice to using GPS for precision flooding. This is just the next step in protecting what your family’s built. Small changes (like better drainage or staggered harvests) can make a big difference without breaking tradition.
